    Names from history

    Three people from American history are connected to Gary's side. Each card says how, and how sure I am about it.

    Probably a cousin

    Daniel Boone

    1734–1820

    Frontiersman. He cut the road into Kentucky and helped found Boonesborough in 1775.

    How you're related

    Your 8th great-grandfather, Josiah Boone, was Daniel Boone's first cousin. Their fathers, George and Squire Boone, were brothers. That makes Daniel your first cousin ten times removed. The "removed" part counts the generations between Josiah and you.

    You're actually related to the Boones twice. Two sisters, Leah and Hannah Boone, are both your ancestors. Leah's son Christopher Karn married Hannah Ann Bristow, who was probably Hannah's granddaughter. Going that way, Daniel is also your first cousin eleven times removed.

    How sure

    Pretty sure, but it isn't proven. Records cover most of the chain. For two links I don't have a record that says it outright: that Josiah Boone (born 1758) was the son of the Josiah born in 1726, and that the older Josiah was the son of George Boone (born 1690). Land deeds, wills and DNA matches all point that way.

    Probably a direct ancestor

    Capt. Alexander Culbertson

    born about 1714, killed 1756

    A militia captain on the Pennsylvania frontier. In April 1756 he led the chase after a war party that had burned Fort McCord, and he was killed at Sideling Hill with many of his men.

    How you're related

    If this holds up, he's your 8th great-grandfather. The line runs from him through the Culbertson, Long and Pickard families down to Gary's mom, Minnie Kathryn Smithson.

    How sure

    Probably. I can get back to John Culbertson, who died in Muhlenberg County, Kentucky, in 1844, with records. The only thing that says John was the captain's son is a Culbertson family history printed in 1923. Other records fit, since the captain did have a son named John, but none of them says it's the same John.

    Related by marriage

    Abraham Lincoln

    1809–1865

    The 16th president of the United States, born in a log cabin in Kentucky in 1809.

    How you're related

    Not by blood. In 1748 William Boone married Sarah Lincoln in Pennsylvania. William was a brother of your 8th great-grandfather, Josiah Boone, and Sarah was a sister of the president's great-grandfather, John Lincoln.

    The two families were neighbors in Exeter, Pennsylvania. The 1736 will of Mordecai Lincoln, the president's great-great-grandfather, calls George Boone, your 9th great-grandfather, one of his "loving friends and neighbours" and makes him a trustee.

    How sure

    Probably. A 1909 book on Lincoln's ancestors prints the Quaker meeting's record of the marriage, and wills on both sides fit it. Your side of it depends on the same two Boone links as the Daniel Boone connection.

    My main site also has Gov. Isaac Shelby. He's on my mom's side, so he isn't one of yours.

    Stories from Gary's side

    Your 2nd great-grandfather1894–1961

    John Leigh Smithson

    Gary's grandfather on his mom's side. He was born in Kirkmansville, Kentucky. In World War I he was a corporal in Company B of the 167th Infantry, and he sailed from Hoboken, New Jersey, on 13 January 1918. He died in Owensboro, Kentucky.

    Your 4th great-grandfatherborn about 1823, died 1865

    William B. Smithson

    He was a Confederate soldier in the Civil War and was held prisoner at Point Lookout. He died at Petersburg, Virginia, in June 1865, on his way home.

    Your 4th great-grandmother1823–1913

    Jane Campbell

    William's wife. She was born in Ireland in 1823, probably in County Derry. Her father was probably Archibold Campbell, a tailor who married in Paisley, Scotland, in 1822.

    Jane married William in Lunenburg, Virginia, in 1845. By 1848 her parents were living in Fredericton, New Brunswick, in Canada, and by 1860 they were back in Virginia. Jane outlived William by 48 years and died in Christian County, Kentucky. Her obituary names her eight children.

    Your 5th great-grandfather

    Felicien Smith

    Born in Alsace, which is now part of France. On 12 June 1834 he landed in New York on a ship called the Mazeppa. He's listed as Felizian Schmidt, age 30, from Baden. Schmidt is German for Smith.

    His great-granddaughter Juanita Kathleen Smith was Gary's grandmother.

    Stories that didn't hold up

    A lot of family trees online are copied from each other, mistakes and all. These claims were in older trees for Gary's side. I checked each one against the records.

    Records say no

    The Smithson baronets

    An old tree ran the Smithsons up to the baronets of Stanwick in England, relatives of the Dukes of Northumberland. In that tree one mother would have been 12 years old, a man named Francis Marion was born decades before General Marion, and Louisa County, Virginia, shows up before the county existed. None of it had a source.

    Records found the right man

    The wrong William

    Older trees had a different man in this spot: William Stanton Johnson, born in Todd County, Kentucky, in 1854, from a family said to go back to a "Squire James Johnson" of Staffordshire, England. Records show your 4th great-grandfather, William S. Johnson, was born about 1846. His father was Paul Dandridge Johnson of Franklin County.

    Not proven

    The Lady of Allington

    A chain of names copied from tree to tree runs one family up to Elizabeth Wyatt, the "Lady of Allington" Castle in Kent, England. The records I have stop at your 8th great-grandfather, Watson Patmon. I haven't found one that names the mother of his daughter Cynthia.

    Doesn't add up

    "Scotch" Culbertson

    Older trees take the Culbertsons back past the captain to a William "Scotch" Culbertson, who supposedly died at Shippensburg, Pennsylvania, in 1709. That's about twenty years before the town was settled. The picture attached to him is a Revolutionary War pension file.

    Documented or probable

    I mark every ancestor one of two ways.

    Documented
    A record shows the link, like a birth or death record, a census, a will, a deed or a marriage record.
    Probable
    The records point that way, but none says it outright yet. On the map these links are dashed, and in the lists they're marked probable.

    On Gary's side, 80 of the 138 ancestors are documented and 58 are probable. DNA helps too. I've taken a DNA test, and a lot of these links are backed up by distant cousins who share my DNA and descend from the same couples.
